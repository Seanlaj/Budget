How the app works, in one minute

You decide on a handful of categories (for example Groceries, Gas, Eating Out) and how much you want to spend on each one every month. That amount is the category's monthly allowance.

Every purchase is recorded as an expense with a category. The app adds them up and shows how much is left in each category for the month. A new month starts fresh automatically.

Expenses get into the app in two ways:

  • Automatically from your connected bank and credit cards (the app checks for new purchases every hour), or
  • By hand, using the green + button.
Good habitOpen the app once or twice a week, tap Review if it appears, and confirm the categories. It only takes a minute and keeps your numbers accurate.
Private and secureOnly your approved email address can open this app, and bank connections are read-only, so nothing can ever move your money. Section 15 explains exactly how your information is protected.

4. First-time setup: create your categories

Your app starts empty, so the first step is to create your categories. You can change them any time.

  1. Tap ⚙ in the top-right corner to open Settings.
  2. Find the section Categories & monthly allowances.
  3. In the last row, type a name in New category (for example Groceries), enter the monthly amount, and tap Add.
  4. Repeat for each category.
  5. Tap ← in the top-right corner to go back to the main screen.

Ideas to get started

Groceries · Eating Out · Gas · Car Maintenance · Healthcare · Utilities · Phone · Internet · Insurance · Mortgage or Rent · Clothes · Gifts · Giving · Entertainment · Miscellaneous

Include a "Miscellaneous" categoryThe app uses Miscellaneous Needs as its fallback for bank purchases it can't place. If you create a category with exactly that name, those purchases land there and wait for your review.

Changing a category later

  • Rename it or change the amount: edit the name or allowance in its row, then tap Save. Renaming also updates every past expense in that category.
  • Stop using a category: uncheck Shown and tap Save. It disappears from the budget and the pickers, but past expenses are kept. Check it again to bring it back.

Let the app suggest categories

After you connect a bank (next section), Settings has a Suggested new categories section. Tap Analyze bank history. The app looks at the last 90 days of bank activity, finds regular spending your categories don't cover yet (mortgage, insurance, utilities and so on), and proposes a monthly amount for each.

Check the ones you want, adjust the names and amounts if you like, and tap Add selected categories. Your existing categories are never changed by this.

5. Connecting your bank and credit cards

Once connected, purchases appear in the app automatically about every hour. The connection is read-only: the app can see transactions, but it can never move money or make payments.

  1. Open Settings ⚙ and scroll to Connected accounts.
  2. Tap Connect account. A secure window from Plaid opens. Plaid is the service many finance apps use to connect to banks.
  3. Search for your bank, sign in with your normal online-banking username and password, and approve any security code your bank sends.
  4. Choose the accounts to share (checking, savings, credit cards) and finish.
  5. Back in the app, the bank appears in the list. The first import can take a few minutes. Tap Sync now to check for new transactions right away.

Repeat Connect account for each bank or card company. One bank login can include several accounts.

Some banks send you to their own websiteSome banks, like Chase, take you to their own website or app to approve the connection and then bring you back to the Budget app. That's normal. The app opens Settings automatically to finish the connection.

What gets imported

  • Purchases and payments from the date you connected onward (starting from the 1st of that month).
  • Card purchases usually appear within a few hours, marked with an amber Pending tag until the bank finishes processing them (usually 1–3 days). The amount can still change, for example when a restaurant tip is added, and the app updates it automatically. Any category you've already chosen is kept.
  • Paying off a credit card, transfers between your own accounts, and deposits are not counted as spending. That way nothing is counted twice.

Keeping connections healthy

  • If a bank shows "Bank login expired — reconnect to resume syncing" (usually after a password change), tap Reconnect and sign in again.
  • To stop syncing a bank, tap Disconnect. Expenses already imported stay in the app.

6. Adding an expense by hand

Use this for cash purchases or anything that isn't on a connected account.

  1. Tap the round green + in the bottom-right corner.
  2. Fill in Date, Amount, Store, Items (a short description) and choose a Category.
  3. Tap Add expense. The window closes and the expense appears in the list.
Paid by card that's already connected?Then you don't need to add it by hand. It will come in automatically. If you do add it by hand too, the app notices the likely duplicate and asks you about it (see section 7).

7. Reviewing imported transactions

The app places most bank purchases in a category by itself, and it gets smarter as you use it. When it isn't sure, it marks the transaction Needs review and an amber Review 3 button appears above your expenses.

  1. Tap Review. A short list opens with each transaction's store, amount and date.
  2. For each one, pick the right category and tap Save.
  3. When the list is empty it says All caught up. Close it with ×.
The app learnsWhen you choose a category for a store, the app remembers it. Next time that store shows up, it's filed automatically. Stores like Walmart, Target and Amazon are always sent to review, because they sell many kinds of things.

"Possible duplicate of a manual entry"

If a bank purchase matches something you already entered by hand (same amount, within a few days), the app flags it. You'll see two choices:

  • Keep: it's a different purchase. Keep both.
  • Delete: it's the same purchase. Removes the bank copy and keeps yours.

8. Changing a transaction's category

For any bank transaction, tap its gray category label (for example Groceries) in the list. Pick a new category and tap Save. The app also remembers this choice for that store from now on.

9. Splitting one payment into several categories

Sometimes a single payment covers several things, like a Costco trip that was part groceries and part household items. You can split it.

  1. In the expense list, tap ✂ (scissors) on that row.
  2. For each part, choose a category and type an amount. Tap + Add part for more parts (up to 6). Use rest fills in whatever is left.
  3. The bottom line must say Fully assigned before you can save. It shows how much is left or how much you're over.
  4. Tap Save split. The parts appear as separate rows tagged Split.

Repeat payments, split the same way every time

For a bank payment that repeats every month (for example one transfer that covers two bills), leave Split future payments to this payee the same way checked. Next month the app splits it for you. The last part always gets whatever is left over, in case the total changes a little.

To undo a split, tap ✂ again and choose Undo split.

10. Searching and sorting

Right above the expense list:

  • Search box: type part of a store name or description (for example costco). The list shows only matching expenses, and the count shows "X of Y transactions." Clear the box to see everything again.
  • Sort by: Date (newest first), Date (oldest first), Category, Store, or Amount (largest first).

11. Finding and removing duplicates

If something might have been entered twice, tap Find duplicates above the list. The app checks the month you're looking at for entries with the same amount and a similar store name, dated within 3 days of each other.

For each possible duplicate group:

  • Check the entry or entries to keep (the bank copy is checked for you), then tap Keep checked, delete 1. You'll be asked to confirm.
  • Or tap Not duplicates if they're really separate purchases. The app won't flag them again.

12. Deleting an expense

Tap × at the end of a row and confirm. This can't be undone. Deleting a split payment's original row deletes all of its parts.

13. Looking at past months

Use the ‹ and › arrows at the top. Everything on the screen (totals, balances and the expense list) changes to that month. You can't move past the current month.

14. Dark mode

Tap ☾ at the top to switch to a dark screen, which is easier on the eyes at night. Tap ☀ to switch back. Each phone or computer remembers its own choice.

15. Is my information safe?

Yes. The app was built with security as the first priority, using the same kind of protection banks and large companies use. Here is how your information is kept private.

Only you can get in

  • The app is locked to your email address. Only 238balaj@gmail.com is on the approved list. Anyone else who opens the link sees only a sign-in page and is turned away. Nothing about your budget is shown to them.
  • A new code every time you sign in. There's no password that could be guessed or stolen. Each time you sign in, a new one-time 6-digit code is emailed to you, and it expires after a few minutes.
  • Two separate locks. Cloudflare checks your sign-in before the app even loads. The app then checks again on every request, so your information is only shown when both agree that you're approved.
  • Completely separate from anyone else's budget. Your app has its own private database. Sean's budget lives in a different app and database. Neither family can see the other's information, and Sean can't open yours either.

Your bank accounts stay protected

  • Read-only. The bank connection can only see transactions. It cannot move money, make payments, transfer funds or change anything at your bank.
  • Your bank password is never given to this app. You type it into Plaid's secure window or your bank's own website, never into the Budget app itself. Plaid is used by thousands of well-known finance apps.
  • You stay in control. You can disconnect a bank at any time in Settings. You can also remove access from your bank's own website or app, in its security or connected-apps settings.

Your information is encrypted

  • Encrypted in transit. Everything between your phone and the app travels over an encrypted connection (the padlock in your browser's address bar).
  • Encrypted at rest. The keys that let the app read your bank transactions are stored encrypted with a secret key unique to your app.
  • Hosted on Cloudflare. The app runs on Cloudflare, one of the largest internet security companies in the world, which protects millions of websites.
Your part: two simple habits 1. Never share your 6-digit sign-in code with anyone. The app and Sean will never ask you for it.
2. Keep your email account secure (a strong password and 2-step verification), because sign-in codes are sent there.

16. Questions and troubleshooting

The category list is empty when I add an expense.

Create your categories first in Settings (section 4).

A purchase I made today isn't showing up.

Most card purchases show up within a few hours, marked Pending until the bank finishes processing them. Some banks take longer to report new purchases. You can tap Sync now in Settings to check right away.

A purchase went into the wrong category.

Tap its category label in the list and choose the right one (section 8). The app will remember for next time.

The same purchase shows up twice.

Use Find duplicates (section 11) or delete one copy with ×.

A bank says "login expired."

Open Settings and tap Reconnect next to that bank (section 5).

The page says "Forbidden" or won't load my expenses.

Your sign-in may have expired. Close the app completely, open it again and sign in with your email code. If it keeps happening, contact Sean.

I want to add another person (for example a second email).

Ask Sean. He can approve another email address for the app.
